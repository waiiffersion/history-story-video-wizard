# Story promise — fictional training material

1. Universal pressure question: How do you prove loyalty when the evidence in front of everyone looks bad?
2. Protagonist and starting position: a newly appointed steward inherits an almost empty storehouse.
3. What the protagonist wants: to keep the appointment and make the inventory understandable.
4. Relationship plus anomalous action: the new steward opens the storehouse and finds the old administration's bins empty.
5. Escalation: a later account turns the shortage into an accusation against the steward.
6. Choice: not established; the narration must not invent one.
7. Cost: suspicion attaches to the steward; no punishment may be claimed.
8. Familiar belief: an accusation plus an empty storehouse proves theft.
9. Evidence-led revision: the records prove shortage and blame, not who removed the supplies.
10. Promise: after listening, the audience will understand why a vivid accusation can outlive the evidence that should test it.
